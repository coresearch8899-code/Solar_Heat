# Former Supplementary Material

Tables and figures of the Supplementary Material that accompanied the first version of the article. They are not part of the submitted article; they are kept here as reference outputs. Every value can be regenerated with `run_all.sh`.

- `TableS1.csv`: Table S1. Characteristics of the three PV zones and of the reconstructed 30-min dataset (3 March 2023 to 7 September 2026).
  - Note: a Intervals in which all three zones and the model-attributed temperature-driven load estimate were valid at the same time (60,430 intervals; full-record sample). Sequence in each zone: raw records − duplicated time stamps = unique records; re-indexing to the complete 30-min grid adds empty intervals to give the expected intervals; rejected intervals include those with missing counter values; valid intervals = expected − rejected. The hospital series requires all three zones to be valid (60,444 intervals), and 60,430 of these also have a model estimate (the other 14 lack one or more model features). The 42 analysis months used for the peak and cost metrics are the calendar months with more than 25 days of valid hospital-level data (Table S12). b After correction of grid import and export for the current-transformer ratio error (Section 2.1 of the main article).
- `FigS1.png`: Fig. S1. Operating record, 3 March 2023 to 7 September 2026. (a) Daily gross load L and PV generation E (thin lines: daily values; bold lines: 14-day centred mean). (b) Daily maximum ambient temperature, with heatwave days (≥ 3 consecutive days at or above the 90th percentile, 35.5 °C) marked.
- `TableS2.csv`: Table S2. Performance of the temperature-sensitivity models.
  - Note: Square brackets give 95% weekly block-bootstrap confidence intervals. a Partial years (2023 from 3 March, the commissioning year; 2026 to 7 September). ASHRAE Guideline 14 hourly criteria: CV(RMSE) < 30%, |NMBE| < 10% [21,28].
- `TableS3.csv`: Table S3. Interpretation of the Solar-Heat Hedge Ratio and its relation to conventional indicators.
- `TableS4.csv`: Table S4. Conventional load-match indicators, solar-heat hedge indicators and peak-protection metrics by season and year.
  - Note: Seasons: cool-dry, November–February; hot-dry, March–May; wet, June–October. SC: self-consumption; SS: self-sufficiency; SF: solar fraction; RF: renewable fraction (ΣE/ΣL); LMI: mean 30-min load cover. Temperature-driven quantities refer to the model-attributed temperature-driven load (reference model). Peak metrics refer to the 30-min peak-demand proxy and use the 42 analysis months only. Values are computed on the modelling sample with the reference model refitted in step 07 (seasonal refit) and with SC as Σmin(E, L)/ΣE; they differ from Table 2 of the main article by up to 0.5 percentage points (largest in the partial year 2023). Peak-proxy reductions in % are means of the monthly ratios. a Partial years.
- `TableS5.csv`: Table S5. Daily hedge performance by daily maximum temperature (hospital, all days).
- `FigS2.png`: Fig. S2. Heat stratification of the hedge. (a) SHHR under heat-priority and proportional allocation by daily Tmax class, with 95% confidence intervals. (b) Mean daily peak reduction by PV. (c) Diurnal distribution of model-attributed temperature-driven energy, divided into the part covered by coincident PV under heat priority and the uncovered hedge gap.
- `TableS6.csv`: Table S6. Heatwave days compared with matched reference days (n = 101 pairs).

  - Note: Months with at least 90% data coverage. a Interquartile range. A ratio of 30-min to billed 15-min peak below 1 is expected, because a 30-min average cannot exceed the 15-min maximum of the same load.

  - Note: Portal import was scaled to the full month by the fraction of valid intervals, and F was inferred as (invoice total/1.07 − energy, demand and service charges)/billed energy, using the portal on-peak share.
- `TableS9.csv`: Table S9. Stress-test results interpolated at IPCC AR6 warming increments (present fleet, k = 1).
  - Note: Global best estimates are taken from [11]. Increments are relative to 2011–2020 (1.1 °C) and are used as a proxy for local warming; values are interpolated from the uniform-shift stress test and are indicative only. The SSP5-8.5 late-century increment exceeds the tested range (3.0 °C), so only bounds are given.

- `FigS4.png`: Fig. S4. Change in the mean monthly 30-min on-peak net demand proxy relative to the present over warming ΔT and PV scale factor k for the six load-model families: (a) monotone gradient boosting (reference), (b) gradient boosting with short lags, (c) unconstrained gradient boosting, (d) additive spline model, (e) linear hourly cooling-degree model and (f) hourly change-point model. - `FigS5.png`: Fig. S5. Synthesis of the findings. The measured peak displacement and the attribution-independent preservation multipliers (k*imp, k*peak) form the main evidence; the model-attributed temperature-driven load, thermal lag and solar-heat hedge explain them; late-afternoon flexibility is shown as a complementary option.
